02 — Gradient anatomy

Never interpolate peach → plum through grey.

A two-stop blend in RGB passes through a dusty rose-brown that reads as a bruise. The fix is routing the hue down through red instead: coral, then rose, then wine. Same destination, alive the whole way.

04 — The bite

Two scallops. One direction.

The bite is two arcs of the same radius — teeth, not damage. The needle points at the centre of the bite, always. This is the entire joke; don't explain it further.

Geometric grotesque, lowercase, tracking −3.5%. One letter carries two scalloped notches — that's the whole detail. The bitten "o" takes the accent colour; the rest stays ink or blush.

07 — Splash screen

The needle is the loading state.

No spinner. The compass wobbles while data loads, then snaps to 35° when ready. The dial flinches once — the chomp.

11 — Guardrails

The restraint is the sophistication.

The moment you illustrate the joke, you lose the App Store, the brand deal, and the cool.

✓

Keep the needle pointing into the bite

That's the entire joke. If it points away, the mark becomes a diet app with a chip in it.

✓

Keep the bite at two scallops

Two notches read as teeth. Three read as a gear. One reads as damage.

✓

Let fig live in the shadow, not the mass

Plum belongs in the deepest gradient stop, the noir surface, and one secondary accent. Past roughly 40% of the ring, it stops reading as fruit.

✓

Route peach → plum through red

Coral and rose in the middle stops keep the transition alive. A straight two-stop blend lands in mud.

✕

Don't add a face, a tongue, or a wink

The innuendo is carried by a piece of fruit and a bite mark. Literal illustration kills it.

✕

Don't rotate the mark

The bite lives at 1–2 o'clock. That's the fixed position across every application.

✕

Don't put plum in the wordmark's bitten "o"

It's the smallest, most literal joke in the system. Peach there; plum would over-explain it.

✕

Don't use purple-grey as a neutral

The neutrals are warm plum-black and blush-white. Desaturated lavender-grey reads cosmetic, not edible.
